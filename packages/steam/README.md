# Steam

**Steam** is a digital game distribution platform and desktop gaming client developed by **Valve**. It lets you discover, purchase, download, install, launch, and update PC games from one application. Steam also provides a library for managing your games and connecting with other players.

## Features

- **Game store:** Browse a large catalog of paid and free-to-play games, including titles from independent developers and major publishers.
- **Game library:** Install, organize, launch, and automatically update your games.
- **Community:** Connect with friends, chat, join groups, and participate in discussions.
- **Steam Cloud:** Synchronize supported game saves and settings across devices.
- **Steam Workshop:** Find and manage community-created modifications and content for supported games.
- **Steam Remote Play:** Stream supported games to other devices and play compatible local multiplayer games online with friends.
- **Controller support:** Play supported games with controllers using Steam Input and Big Picture Mode.

## Gaming on Linux

Steam has a native Linux client and offers games with native Linux support. **Steam Play**, powered by **Proton**, can also run many Windows games on Linux. Compatibility varies by game, hardware, graphics drivers, and anti-cheat software; not every Windows game will work.

## Getting Started

After installing Steam, open the application and sign in with a Steam account, or create one for free. You can then browse the store, download games, and launch them from your library. An internet connection is required for account setup and downloads; offline play is available for some installed games.

**Steam is free to install**, but individual games and other content may require payment. Steam is proprietary software.
